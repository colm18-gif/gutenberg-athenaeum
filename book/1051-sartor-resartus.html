Sartor Resartus

by Thomas Carlyle

Carlyle’s strange book, first printed in Fraser’s Magazine in 1833 and 1834, pretends to be an English editor’s account of a German professor’s treatise on clothes, Die Kleider, ihr Werden und Wirken. The professor, Diogenes Teufelsdröckh of the University of Weissnichtwo (“Know-not-where”), is as imaginary as his book. Through them Carlyle argues that everything people make, from coats to churches and states, is clothing for something underneath.
