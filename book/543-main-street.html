Main Street

by Sinclair Lewis

A woman with ambitions for culture and reform moves to a small prairie town and discovers the town has rather firm opinions about being improved. Lewis skewered small-town American complacency so precisely that the book briefly became a genuine social scandal. She does not win, exactly, but she also never entirely stops trying.
