Napoleon’s Appeal to the British Nation

by Emperor Napoleon

In 1816 Napoleon’s complaints about how he was kept at Longwood, the house, the guards, the money and the governor, were set down in a long letter signed by General Montholon, who had gone into exile with him. It was smuggled to London and printed there in 1817 as an appeal to the British people. It is the captivity as the captive saw it, and the governor never forgave it.
