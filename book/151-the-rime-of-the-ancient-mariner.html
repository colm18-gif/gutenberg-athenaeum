The Rime of the Ancient Mariner

by Samuel Taylor Coleridge

A sailor shoots an albatross for no particular reason and is made, by the ship and the sea and possibly the universe itself, to understand exactly what that carelessness cost. He tells the story now to strangers at weddings, compulsively, because telling it is the only penance available to him. Coleridge wrote the guilt more vividly than the crime, which is rather the correct proportion.
