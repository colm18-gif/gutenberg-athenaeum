Polite Conversation in Three Dialogues

by Jonathan Swift

Swift collected the stale phrases of fashionable talk for years and published them in 1738 as three dialogues, in which ladies and gentlemen trade proverbs and pleasantries from the morning visit to the card table. It is presented by an invented editor, Simon Wagstaff, who is proud of every one of them. The dialogues are a record of how the gentry of his day really spoke, which is what makes them so unkind.
