Carlyle’s Laugh, and Other Surprises

by Thomas Wentworth Higginson

Higginson’s late essays, of 1909, and the one to read first is the chapter called Emily Dickinson. It prints her letters to him, beginning with the first, and tells of his visit to Amherst in 1870, when she came into the parlour with two day-lilies in her hand. He never quite knew what to make of her, and is honest enough to say so.
