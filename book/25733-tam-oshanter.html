Tam O’Shanter

by Robert Burns

Tam, a farmer who stays too long at the inn in Ayr on market night, rides home through a storm past Alloway Kirk and sees through its window warlocks and witches dancing to the Devil’s pipes. Burns wrote it in 1790 for Francis Grose’s Antiquities of Scotland, as the tale to go with a drawing of the kirk, and called it his standard performance in the poetical line. Tam escapes over the bridge, but his mare Meg leaves her tail behind.
