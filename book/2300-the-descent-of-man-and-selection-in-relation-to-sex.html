The Descent of Man, and Selection in Relation to Sex

by Charles Darwin

Twelve years after the Origin, Darwin said plainly what he had left almost unsaid there: that people, too, are descended from earlier animals. Most of the book is about sexual selection, the choosing of mates, which he thought explained the peacock’s tail and a good deal of human difference besides. His guess that humanity began in Africa, made from the apes then living there, has held.
