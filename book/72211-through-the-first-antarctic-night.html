Through the First Antarctic Night

by Frederick A. Cook

Frederick Cook was the doctor of the Belgica, the Belgian ship caught in the pack ice west of the Antarctic Peninsula in 1898, which drifted through the winter: the first expedition to spend one in the Antarctic. Her first mate was a young Roald Amundsen. Cook’s account, published in 1900, is a record of what the long dark did to the men, and of the fresh meat he and Amundsen pressed on them against the scurvy.
